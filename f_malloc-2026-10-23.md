# udp_rx_mode / tcp_dispatch_mode with F_MALLOC: re-run on 2026-10-23

More F_MALLOC data for the three receive modes, from runs on 2026-10-23 on the setup of sections
[4.6](README.md#46-call-rate-capacity-cps-through-a-stateful-proxy) and
[4.7](README.md#47-call-rate-capacity-over-tls-and-wss) of the [README](README.md): 8 workers per
transport, Linux 6.12, 30-second cells, two runs per rate and mode (ABC then CBA). A rate holds when
both runs complete at least 98% of the offered calls with at most 0.1% failed. The build is a 4.1
tree with [OpenSIPS PR #4365](https://github.com/OpenSIPS/opensips/pull/4365) (receive-mode code as in e2b14273cd) and other pending patches, and every cell ran
with `-a F_MALLOC -m 2048 -M 32`. Besides the stateful relay of 4.6, I also tested a load balancer:
`dispatcher` round-robin over 12 UAS, with either `dialog` or topology hiding (the dialog-less
topology hiding of OpenSIPS #4114, with its state in `cachedb_perf` (#4118) in process memory;
neither is merged).

* `shared`: the load rises with fork order in every row (rank correlation with fork order +0.8 to
  +1.0 per row): the ladder the PR set out to remove. On the UDP relay it flattens as the load
  rises, from 8% at 8,000/s to 2-4% from 12,000/s; the PR body's OPTIONS bench at 1,000 datagrams/s
  showed 55%.
* `exclusive`: the opposite order, the first-forked worker busiest (-0.8 to -1.0 per row), and the
  widest spread in every row: up to 24% on UDP and 60-77% on TCP, where the last-forked TCP worker
  averaged 7% (TLS) and 3% (WSS).
* `auto`: 0-3% in every row, with no consistent order (rank correlation -0.2 to +0.7).
* Near saturation (LB + dialog at 8,000/s, workers at 72-90%) all three modes stay at 2-5%.

## Reading

* `exclusive` loads the workers in a fixed order: a CPU spread of 5-24% on UDP and 60-77% across
  the TCP workers (the spread table above). Over TLS/WSS it is the cheapest per call (0.8% and
  3.4% below `auto`).
  The TCP ceilings hardly differ: TLS 6,000 in every mode, WSS 5,000 / 5,000 / 6,000.
* On UDP, `auto` costs the least CPU per call (on the relay, 0.5% more than `exclusive`), with no
  empty reads and the fewest context switches (tied with `exclusive` on the relay). It dropped
  nothing in these rows; `shared` and `exclusive` dropped datagrams in two of the three.
* A caveat on drops: in `auto` every worker has its own socket with the full buffer, so the kernel
  can queue eight times as much as with the one shared socket. Part of `auto`'s lower drop counts,
  and of its edge at the ceilings, can come from that; I did not run `auto` with an eighth of the
  buffer to separate the two. The per-call figures count calls as datagrams taken in divided by 6,
  and in the overload rows every queue was full, so those hinge on it far less.
* Past the ceiling, `auto` took in 1.7-2.0 times as many datagrams per second as `shared` and
  `exclusive` on the same CPU, at 2.7-4 times fewer syscalls per datagram. I did not split these
  syscalls by type in these runs. In the per-syscall cells of 4.6, most F_MALLOC syscalls were
  `sched_yield` on the shared-memory lock.
